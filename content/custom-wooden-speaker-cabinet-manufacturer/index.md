## Overview

Woodsat is a **custom wooden speaker cabinet manufacturer** working with loudspeaker brands, contract audio developers, studio equipment companies and installation specialists who need enclosures built to a specification rather than pulled from a catalogue. Every project we take on begins with a set of constraints — an internal volume, a driver footprint, a tuning frequency, a finish standard and a landed cost target — and our job is to reconcile those constraints into a cabinet that can be manufactured repeatably, thousands of times, without drifting away from the reference sample you approved.

The word *custom* covers a wide range in this industry. For some clients it means a straightforward build-to-print job: complete CAD, a validated bill of materials and a finish reference, where our contribution is disciplined execution. For others it means starting from a sketch and a pair of drivers, working through internal volume calculations, bracing layouts, port geometry and cosmetic detailing until there is something worth tooling. Woodsat is structured to handle both. Our engineering team reads acoustic intent, not just dimensions, and will tell you when a wall thickness, a joint type or a veneer choice is going to cause problems in production or in the measured response.

Everything happens inside one facility. Enclosure engineering, CNC panel machining, edge banding and joinery, veneer lay-up, spray finishing in filtered rooms, damping installation, acoustic verification, hardware fitting and export packing all sit under the same roof and the same quality system. That vertical integration matters more than it sounds. When veneering, finishing and machining are split across three subcontractors, tolerance stack-up and colour drift become somebody else's problem — and eventually yours. Keeping the chain internal is how we hold a baffle cut-out to a fraction of a millimetre and hold a high-gloss black to a consistent gloss unit across an entire production run.

## Why a Wooden Cabinet Still Wins

Wood-based panel products remain the reference material for serious loudspeakers because they can be *tuned*. Density, thickness, bracing pitch and internal damping are all variables an engineer can move, and each one changes where panel resonances sit and how quickly they decay. A moulded polymer housing gives you shape freedom and low unit cost at very high volume, but it hands you a fixed resonant signature you then have to fight with ribs and mastic. With MDF, plywood or hardwood, the enclosure becomes part of the acoustic design rather than an obstacle to it.

There is a commercial argument as well. A wooden cabinet reads as permanent and considered, particularly at the price points where customers audition before buying. Book-matched grain flowing across a baffle, a mitred corner with no visible end grain, a lacquer surface with genuine depth — these are the details that let a brand hold a premium position. Woodsat builds cabinets for both sides of that equation: the measurable performance the engineering team cares about, and the tactile quality the marketing team is selling.

A programme starts with your drawings, a co-development brief, or in many cases a physical reference cabinet you want improved. We build the 3D model, confirm net internal volume after bracing and driver displacement, and check panel stiffness where large unsupported spans exist. Once the geometry is signed off, panels are nested and machined on multi-axis routers so that every part carries its own joinery — no hand-fitting on the line, which is the single biggest source of variation in wooden cabinet production.

Veneer is laid up and pressed, edges are wrapped or banded so no substrate shows, and the cabinet is sanded through progressive grits before entering the spray rooms. Finishing is where schedules are won or lost: a full piano-lacquer build involves multiple sealer, colour and clear coats with cure and flat-sanding between each, followed by machine polishing. After finishing, damping material is installed to the documented specification, the cabinet is acoustically checked, hardware is fitted, and units are packed in transit-tested cartons ready for consolidation and export.

| Bamboo ply | Renewable, distinctive grain, high hardness | Eco-positioned and design-led products |
| Bending ply / laminated stock | Formable, supports curved side walls | Curved-wall cabinets with reduced internal standing waves |

Material choice is never purely acoustic. A 25 mm MDF cabinet may measure beautifully and still fail commercially because airfreight cost doubles. We routinely model the trade-off between wall thickness, bracing density and shipping weight so the finished product is defensible on both the test bench and the cost sheet.

## Finishes & Veneers

We offer natural wood veneer across a wide range of species — walnut, oak, ash, sapele, ebony, rosewood substitutes and dyed reconstituted veneers — laid up book-matched so the grain mirrors across the baffle or centre line. On the paint side, capability spans high-gloss piano lacquer with full flat-and-polish, matte and satin PU systems, soft-touch coatings, metallic and pearlescent effects, and textured finishes that hide handling marks in commercial installations. Mixed constructions are common: a painted body with a veneered baffle, or a matte cabinet with a gloss top panel.

Colour control is handled against a physical master panel approved by the customer, with production units checked under standardised lighting. For programmes with multiple SKUs in the same colour family, we lock the coating batch and spray parameters so a soundbar and a matching subwoofer shipped six months apart still look like one product family on a shelf.

## Quality & Acoustic Control

Every cabinet is built to a controlled bill of materials and a documented process sheet. Incoming panel stock is checked for thickness tolerance, flatness and moisture content, because wood-based materials that arrive wet will move after finishing no matter how good the machining is. In process, first-article parts are dimensionally verified against the CAD model and re-checked at set intervals through the run. Glue lines, squareness and internal bracing placement are inspected before the cabinet is closed, since nothing inside can be corrected afterwards.

Acoustically, we verify enclosure airtightness, confirm damping quantity and placement against the specification, and measure representative units so port tuning and in-box response track the approved reference. For stereo products, left and right units are matched and shipped as pairs. All measurement records, coating batch numbers and material certificates are retained against the production lot, so if a question arises twelve months later there is a traceable answer rather than a guess.

## OEM and ODM Programmes

Woodsat supports confidential **OEM and ODM speaker cabinet programmes**. Under OEM we manufacture to your drawings, your brand and your packaging, adding manufacturability feedback but leaving the design authority with you. Under ODM we take a broader role: proposing enclosure architecture, selecting materials and finishes, developing samples and carrying the product through to stable series production. Non-disclosure agreements, controlled drawing revisions, restricted tooling access and serialised traceability protect your intellectual property throughout.

Commercially we are set up for the realities of the audio business — pilot runs before full launch, phased tooling investment, staged shipments against a launch calendar, and safety stock of long-lead veneer or hardware so a single material shortage does not stall a product introduction.
