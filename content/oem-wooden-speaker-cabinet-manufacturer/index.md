## Overview

Choosing an OEM partner for wooden speaker cabinets is a decision about risk as much as about price. The cabinet is the largest, heaviest, most visible part of a loudspeaker; if it arrives with a colour drift, a soft glue line or a driver recess a millimetre out, the whole product is unsellable and the launch date moves. Woodsat operates as an **OEM wooden speaker cabinet manufacturer** for brands who need that risk managed properly — through documented process control, disciplined sample approval and honest communication about what is and is not achievable at the target cost.

Our OEM work splits into two modes. Build-to-print OEM means you own the design and we execute it exactly, contributing manufacturability feedback but changing nothing without a written engineering change. ODM means we contribute design work: proposing enclosure architecture, materials, finishes and construction methods around your brief, then developing the product through samples into series production. Many programmes are a hybrid, where the brand controls industrial design and acoustics and we own the construction detailing and process engineering.

Confidentiality is treated seriously because audio is a design-led business and a leaked cabinet shape is a leaked product. We work under NDA from first enquiry, restrict programme drawings to the personnel who need them, keep customer-specific tooling and jigs controlled and identified, and do not display client work in marketing without written permission. Several products we build carry no visible indication of their origin, which is exactly as it should be.

The programme starts with an NDA and a technical review of your drawings, reference samples or brief. We return a design-for-manufacture assessment alongside the quotation — not a list of objections, but a practical note on which details drive cost, which tolerances are difficult in wood, and where a small geometry change buys a large yield improvement. Once commercial terms are agreed, we build a T1 prototype for your evaluation.

Prototype rounds continue until a golden sample is signed off, at which point that physical sample becomes the reference for the entire programme. Tooling, jigs and spray masks are built to match it, and a pilot run validates that the process produces the sample repeatably rather than by hand-finishing. Series production then runs against fixed work instructions and an inspection plan, with in-process checks at machining, pre-closure, post-finish and final assembly. Finished units are packed in your branded cartons, palletised and consolidated for container shipment.

## Material Options

| Pre-veneered panel | Reduces in-house veneering time and cost | Volume programmes with a consistent wood look |
| Solid hardwood | Highest cost, strongest luxury signal | Flagship and limited-edition SKUs |
| Recycled / FSC-certified stock | Supports sustainability claims and tender requirements | Brands with environmental commitments |

Material strategy on an OEM programme is usually about total landed cost rather than panel price. A slightly more expensive substrate that finishes in fewer coats, or a pre-veneered panel that removes a whole press operation, will often beat the cheaper board once labour, yield and rework are included. We are happy to price two or three constructions side by side so the decision is made with real numbers.

## Finishes & Veneers

For OEM customers, finish consistency across time is more important than any single spectacular sample. Anyone can spray one beautiful gloss cabinet; the difficulty is producing the same gloss level, the same colour and the same orange-peel-free surface across a shipment twelve months later. We control this with approved master panels, locked coating batches and recorded spray parameters, plus standardised lighting at inspection so subjective judgement is minimised.

Available finishes cover book-matched natural veneer, dyed and reconstituted veneer, high-gloss piano lacquer, matte and satin PU, soft-touch coatings, metallic and pearl effects, and durable textured finishes for commercial hardware. Where a brand runs multiple product families in the same colour, we hold a single reference standard across all of them so a subwoofer, a centre channel and a pair of towers look correct when stacked together in a showroom.

## Quality & Acoustic Control

Every OEM programme runs against a written inspection plan agreed before the pilot run. Incoming panel stock is checked for thickness, flatness and moisture content. First articles are dimensionally verified against the CAD model, and in-process checks are scheduled at defined intervals rather than left to operator discretion. Pre-closure inspection covers bracing placement, glue coverage and internal wiring provision. Post-finish inspection covers colour, gloss, surface defects and edge coverage. Final inspection is a full functional and cosmetic check before packing.

Acoustic verification is included where the programme calls for it: airtightness checks, damping weight confirmation, impedance sweeps to confirm port tuning, and pair matching for stereo products. All results, coating batch numbers and material certificates are retained against the lot number. If a retailer raises a complaint eighteen months after shipment, we can identify the batch, the materials and the operators involved rather than guessing.

## Programme Management and Commercial Terms

OEM work is a logistics exercise as much as a manufacturing one. We plan capacity against your launch calendar, hold safety stock of long-lead items such as specific veneer flitches or custom hardware, and support phased shipments so you are not paying to warehouse a full year of inventory on day one. Engineering changes go through a formal notification process with re-sampling where the change affects fit, finish or acoustics — no silent substitutions.

We also understand that a first programme with a new supplier is a trial. Many of our long-running relationships began with a single SKU or a pilot quantity, expanding once the brand had seen the quality records and the on-time delivery performance for themselves.
